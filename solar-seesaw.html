The Solar Seesaw

A seesaw balances around a point that shifts toward the heavier side. In space, two bodies move around their shared barycenter—the center of mass of the system. Explore how the balance point changes from Earth–Moon to Sun–Jupiter to Pluto–Charon.

What to notice

  • The more massive body moves in a smaller circle.
  • The barycenter is inside Earth, not exactly at Earth's center.
  • Both bodies orbit the same point.

Compare the three real systems

SystemMass comparisonBarycenter locationMain idea
Earth–MoonEarth ≈ 81× MoonInside EarthEarth makes a small wobble as the Moon orbits the shared center of mass.
Sun–JupiterSun ≈ 1,047× JupiterInside the Sun, relatively close to its centerThe Sun also moves; Jupiter does not orbit a perfectly stationary Sun.
Pluto–CharonPluto ≈ 8.2× CharonOutside Pluto, between the bodiesBoth bodies orbit a point outside Pluto. The pair is often described as a binary-like system.

These ratios are approximate. Real multi-body systems are more complex because other bodies also affect the center of mass.
