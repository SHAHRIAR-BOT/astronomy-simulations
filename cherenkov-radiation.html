How a neutrino hit becomes blue light

When a neutrino hits an atom in the ice, it makes a charged particle that moves faster than light can travel through ice. The result is a cone of blue Cherenkov light.

Light in ice travels at about 76% of its vacuum speed (refractive index about 1.31). Drag the particle speed below 76% and the cone disappears: the light waves just spread out ahead of the particle with no cone. Above 76% they pile up, like the sonic boom of a supersonic jet. The cone angle is about 41° for a particle moving near light speed.

Same effect in a nuclear reactor pool

Fast electrons from the fuel rods move faster than light does in water, so the water glows blue.
