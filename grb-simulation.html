Gamma-ray bursts: how they start, and why we only see some

Pick a type of burst and watch it unfold. Then tilt the jet to see why a GRB looks astonishingly bright only when a jet points at Earth.

Gamma-ray brightness seen from Earth

The jet opening here is exaggerated (about 10° half-angle) so you can see it. Real jets are narrower still, which is why most GRBs are never pointed at us. The dashed line marks 2 seconds, the usual dividing line between short and long bursts. If a burst went off in our own galaxy with its jet aimed at us, the radiation could damage the ozone layer.
